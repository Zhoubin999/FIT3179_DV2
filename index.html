THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Digital listening grows, but the transition isn't one-size-fits-all.

Follow Australia's audio transformation from evolving listening habits to uneven technology uptake, local radio competition and geographic infrastructure differences. Each figure is a distinct piece of evidence, not a claim that streaming has replaced radio.

Read the evidence carefully.
Radio and streaming can overlap. “Australia overall” includes regional respondents. Different surveys use different populations and reference periods. No map here claims to measure actual reception coverage.
01
CHAPTER 01

What changed—and for whom?

Streaming is growing without making radio disappear. Podcast habits overlap, while older and younger audiences show different radio reach.

The big shift

Radio weekly reach moved from 85% in 2017 to 77% in 2026; streaming audio moved from 46% to 81%. These are overlapping audiences, not competing market shares.

Radio is still widely used by older Australians

Among people aged 55+, weekly radio reach was 80% both nationally and regionally in 2026. The largest national–regional gap was among ages 10–24 (10 percentage points).

CHAPTER 02

Different technologies, different geographies

Online services differ in popularity, and metropolitan and regional areas use broadcasting technologies differently. DAB+ service locations provide geographic context.

Growth is uneven

Spotify was used weekly by 47% in 2026, while YouTube Music reached 29%. The major platforms are not changing at the same rate.

The technology divide

CHAPTER 03

New ways to connect and listen

In-car technology and audio subscriptions are changing access to audio. Local radio station shares show that competition still varies by market.

Digital car connectivity grows unevenly

CarPlay / Android Auto ownership grew strongly after 2019. But the regional figure fell from 47% in 2025 to 40% in 2026, while the nationwide figure stayed at 52%. Each step shows the year-to-year change.

Digital access is not one-size-fits-all

Each grid represents 100 weekly Spotify listeners, read left-to-right from the top row. Paid subscriptions accounted for 59% nationwide versus 56% regionally; shared accounts were more common regionally.

Local markets and infrastructure

Local leaders differ across cities, station rankings evolve and FM retransmission licence structures vary by state. These are different measures—not population coverage.

How strong is each local market leader?

Perth has the largest leading-station share (17.6%) and Brisbane the smallest (12.0%). Colour and labels make the modest difference visible; symbol area still starts at zero.
